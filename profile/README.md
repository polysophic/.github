# Polysophic

**Connect · Organize · Discover · Publish**

Polysophic is a documentation and knowledge publishing initiative focused on composing independently maintained information sources into configurable and cohesive documentation experiences.

Our work explores repository-driven documentation, information architecture, content interoperability and self-hostable knowledge publishing.

## Purpose & Motivation

Polysophic exists to address the fragmentation of knowledge across repositories, teams, projects and information systems.

Documentation frequently belongs to different sources, each with its own ownership, version history, structure and maintenance responsibilities.

The objective is to make distributed knowledge accessible, navigable and understandable without requiring its original sources to be relocated, duplicated or replaced.

## Areas of Focus

- **Documentation Systems:** Structured technical, organizational and project documentation.
- **Repository-Driven Content:** Information maintained through independently versioned repositories.
- **Content Interoperability:** Composing information from different sources and authoring conventions.
- **Information Architecture:** Configurable relationships, collections, hierarchies and navigation.
- **Knowledge Discovery:** Search, filtering, relationships and alternative information representations.
- **Knowledge Publishing:** Configurable, branded and independently hosted documentation experiences.

## Key Initiative Examples

The following examples illustrate selected applications and capabilities explored through our work:

- **Documentation Workspaces:** Configurable environments for organizing documentation projects.
- **Multi-Repository Documentation:** Composing information maintained across independently owned repositories.
- **Content Collections:** Organizing and filtering documentation through configurable information models.
- **Knowledge Discovery:** Exploring relationships, navigation and search across connected content.
- **Branded Publishing:** Creating tailored documentation experiences without modifying canonical content.
- **Self-Hosted Documentation:** Exploring independently managed knowledge publishing environments.

## Approach

Canonical information remains with its original source, while documentation experiences are composed through explicit connections, configuration and presentation rules.

Content can be selected, normalized, organized and presented according to the requirements of individual documentation projects.

Search indexes, navigation structures and other derived information should remain reconstructable from source content and configuration.

## Engineering Principles

- **Source Ownership:** Information should remain with its original owners and repositories.
- **Content Portability:** Documentation should remain accessible independently of its publishing platform.
- **Non-Destructive Composition:** Connect information without unnecessarily duplicating or relocating it.
- **Separation of Content and Presentation:** Allow independent evolution of information and its interface.
- **Rebuildable Derived Information:** Derived structures should be reconstructable from canonical sources.
- **Deployment Independence:** Support environments that can be operated according to different hosting requirements.
